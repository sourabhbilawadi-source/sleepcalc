Melatonin Dosage for Adults: Safety & Timing

Why less is more. Explore the biological mechanics of melatonin, clinical dosing guidelines, and body clock resets.

Sleep Supplements June 18, 2026 ⏱️ 5 min read

Melatonin is one of the most widely used over-the-counter sleep aids in the world. When we cross time zones, work irregular shifts, or struggle with sleep-onset insomnia, reaching for a melatonin supplement seems like a natural, risk-free solution. However, sleep physicians report that the vast majority of adults use melatonin incorrectly—taking doses that are biologically excessive, and timing them in a way that disrupts their internal body clock.

This article analyzes the biology of endogenous melatonin, the clinical reasoning behind the "less is more" dosing rule, and how to schedule your intake for maximum efficacy.

The Biology: Endogenous vs. Exogenous Melatonin

Melatonin is not a sedative or a sleeping pill. Instead, it is a hormone produced by the pineal gland in response to darkness. Its primary job is to signal **circadian darkness** to the brain and body, acting as a chemical starter pistol that initiates the physiological processes needed to transition into sleep (such as cooling core body temperature).

Under normal conditions, your brain secretes only **picogram** amounts of melatonin. When you take a standard 5 mg or 10 mg store-bought tablet, you are introducing blood concentrations that are **50 to 100 times higher** than your natural physiological peak [1]. These supraphysiological doses can desensitize your brain's melatonin receptors, leading to morning grogginess, vivid nightmares, and disrupted sleep architecture.

The Clinical Guideline: The "Less is More" Rule

Clinical sleep trials demonstrate that the optimal dose for resetting the circadian clock or improving sleep onset is between **0.3 mg and 1.0 mg** (a micro-dose) [2]. This low dose is highly effective because it mimics the body's natural hormone surge without overwhelming your receptors. Benefits of micro-dosing include:

  • No Next-Day Grogginess: Low doses are quickly cleared by the liver, ensuring you wake up alert.
  • Receptor Protection: Prevents down-regulation and desensitization of MT1 and MT2 receptors.
  • Natural Melatonin Synthesis: Does not suppress or interfere with your body's natural, endogenous hormone production.

Timing is Everything: The 2-Hour Rule

Taking melatonin right before you turn off the lights is a common mistake. Exogenous melatonin takes time to enter the bloodstream and trigger the circadian shift. To shift your body clock earlier (e.g., to treat delayed sleep phase syndrome or jet lag), take melatonin **1.5 to 2 hours before your target bedtime** [3]. If you want to fall asleep at 11:00 PM, take your micro-dose at 9:00 PM.
