The Science of Melatonin Timing

Unlike traditional sleeping pills which work as sedatives to force sleep, melatonin acts as a chronobiotic—a biological timing signal. It tells your brain's master clock (the suprachiasmatic nucleus) when it is night. Because melatonin works as a clock shifter, when you take it is far more important than how much you take.

How Melatonin Shifts Your Circadian Rhythm

Your internal biological clock produces natural melatonin in response to darkness, peaking in the middle of the night. This onset is called DLMO (Dim Light Melatonin Onset). Taking exogenous melatonin shifts this cycle:

  • Phase Advance (Shift Earlier): Taking melatonin in the late afternoon or early evening (4–6 hours before bedtime) tricks the clock into thinking the night has started early, prompting you to sleep and wake earlier.
  • Phase Delay (Shift Later): Taking melatonin in the morning shifts your clock later, making you sleep and wake later (rarely recommended for standard sleep concerns but useful for some shift schedules).

Why Less is More (The Dosage Paradox)

Many over-the-counter melatonin supplements contain 5 mg to 10 mg per serving. According to clinical sleep specialists, this is a massive overdose that can desensitize your brain receptors, cause vivid nightmares, morning grogginess, and disrupt core body temperature regulation.

Studies show that a microdose of 0.3 mg to 1.5 mg is actually more effective at shifting circadian rhythms and mimics the natural concentrations produced by the pineal gland.

Jet Lag Protocol

When traveling across time zones, your body clock remains synced to your departure city. Re-aligning relies on directional timing:

Eastward Travel: Your day is shortened. To adjust, you need to phase-advance (shift your clock earlier). Take a moderate dose of melatonin at your destination's target bedtime starting on the night of travel.

Westward Travel: Your day is lengthened. Melatonin is rarely needed for Westward travel because it is easier for the human clock to adapt to a longer day. However, if you experience early morning awakenings, taking a tiny dose (0.5 mg) can help you sleep through the morning.
