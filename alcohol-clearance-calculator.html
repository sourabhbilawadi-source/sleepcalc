The Physiology of Alcohol and Sleep

Why alcohol acts as a sleeping aid initially, but destroys sleep quality later.

1. REM Suppression

Alcohol is a potent central nervous system depressant that increases GABA activity, helping you fall asleep fast. However, it severely suppresses REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep in the first half of the night, affecting cognitive function and memory consolidation.

2. Autonomic Disruption

During normal sleep, your parasympathetic nervous system dominates, lowering your heart rate and allowing tissue repair. Alcohol keeps your sympathetic nervous system active, resulting in a higher sleeping heart rate and reduced heart rate variability (HRV).

3. Midnight Rebound

As your liver clears the alcohol (usually at a rate of 0.015% BAC per hour), your body experiences a withdrawal-like stimulant rebound. This causes frequent micro-arousals, nighttime sweating, and early morning wakefulness, leaving you exhausted.
